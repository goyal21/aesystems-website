Both terms get used loosely, so it's worth being precise about what each one actually does.

## What a traditional BMS does

A Building Management System (BMS) is fundamentally a monitoring and scheduling layer. It gives you:

- A dashboard showing what's running, what the current readings are, and whether anything is in an alarm state
- The ability to set schedules ("AHU-3 runs 8am-8pm on weekdays") and fixed setpoints
- Manual override controls, so a facility manager can turn something on or off or change a setpoint by hand

What a BMS doesn't do on its own is *decide* anything. It executes whatever schedule and setpoints a person configured, and it keeps executing them until someone changes them — which, as covered in [how HVAC energy gets wasted](/blog/how-to-reduce-hvac-energy-consumption-commercial-buildings), is exactly where a lot of avoidable energy consumption comes from.

## What an AI-BMS adds

An AI-BMS sits on top of the same equipment — chillers, AHUs, pumps, VFDs — but replaces static schedules and fixed setpoints with a control loop that's continuously adjusting based on real-time conditions: occupancy, outside air temperature, zone demand, chiller loading efficiency, and how equipment is actually performing right now rather than how it was configured to perform when it was commissioned.

Concretely, that means:

- Chiller staging and loading decisions made continuously against actual demand, not fixed on/off schedules
- VFD speeds on pumps and fans actively modulated in real time, not set once and left alone
- Setpoints that adjust within a safe, engineer-approved band instead of drifting from manual changes that never get reverted
- Drift and fault detection that flags issues (a stuck valve, a sensor reading out of range) as they happen, rather than at the next scheduled inspection

AE Systems' own AI-BMS, SAAR, works this way: it optimises chillers, AHUs, pumps and VFDs in a building's *existing* equipment, without requiring any hardware replacement, and typically delivers 20-30% HVAC energy savings on top of what the equipment was achieving under manual or schedule-based control.

## Do you need to replace your BMS to get this?

No — this is the part that surprises most facility managers. An AI-BMS layer is added on top of existing infrastructure, not instead of it. If a building already has a traditional BMS, metering, and controllable equipment (VFDs, actuators, BMS-integrated chillers), an AI-BMS typically integrates with what's already there rather than requiring a rip-and-replace.

The real distinguishing question isn't "do we have a BMS" — most commercial buildings above a certain size already do. It's "is anything actively deciding how our equipment should run right now, or is it just executing a schedule someone set months ago."
