Background: A World-Class Campus with Legacy HVAC

IIT Jammu — one of India's premier technical institutions — runs a rapidly expanding campus in Jagti, Jammu. Like most institutions in India, the campus had invested heavily in HVAC infrastructure: chillers, air handling units (AHUs), fan coil units, and cooling towers installed over several years by different contractors, running on older, rule-based control logic.

The problem wasn't the equipment — it was how the equipment was being run. Fixed schedules, manual setpoints, and zero real-time optimization meant the systems ran at full load even when occupancy was low, temperatures had already been achieved, or outdoor conditions had changed. Energy bills were high. Comfort was inconsistent. And there was no visibility into what was happening across the HVAC network in real time.

The core challenge: HVAC systems accounted for the majority of campus electricity consumption — yet there was no intelligence layer to optimize when, how, and at what load each system should run.

The Solution: SAAR AI-BMS — No Rip-and-Replace

AE Systems, as the authorised national partner of SAAR System Solutions (an IIT Jammu-incubated deep-tech company), proposed a non-invasive retrofit. Rather than replacing any existing HVAC equipment, the team deployed 7 SAAR AI-BMS controllers across the campus — overlaying intelligence on top of the existing infrastructure.

The SAAR controller is an IT-OT converged edge device. It integrates directly with existing building systems via standard protocols (Modbus, BACnet, MQTT) without requiring hardware replacement or extended downtime. Once deployed, it begins learning load patterns, occupancy schedules, and thermal behaviour — then autonomously adjusts setpoints and control signals to minimize energy consumption while maintaining comfort.

What the controllers managed:

  • Chiller plant staging and sequencing — preventing simultaneous peak load across multiple chillers
  • AHU supply air temperature and fan speed modulation
  • Cooling tower fan and pump optimization
  • Occupancy-correlated demand prediction and pre-cooling logic
  • Real-time monitoring dashboard with anomaly detection alerts

Key design principle: The SAAR controller works with what you have. No new chillers. No new AHUs. No new piping. The intelligence is the upgrade — the hardware stays.

Deployment: Phased and Non-Disruptive

The deployment at IIT Jammu was completed in phases to minimise disruption to academic activities. AE Systems' field engineers conducted a detailed site audit first — mapping every HVAC asset, its connected sensors, and existing control wiring — before any controller was installed.

Each SAAR unit was installed within a single working day per zone. The system was commissioned in shadow mode initially, where it monitored and logged data without taking control. This 2–3 week baseline period gave the AI engine enough data to model the building's thermal behaviour before switching to active optimization mode.

Results: 20%+ Energy Savings, Verified

After 60 days of active optimization, the measured results showed consistent energy savings exceeding 20% across the HVAC systems covered by the deployment. The baseline was established from the same period's consumption in the prior year, normalized for occupancy and outdoor temperatures.

Beyond the headline number, the operational benefits were equally significant:

  • Comfort complaints dropped significantly — the AI's predictive pre-cooling meant spaces were at the right temperature before occupants arrived, not 30 minutes after
  • Equipment runtime was reduced — fewer compressor starts, longer off-cycles, and smarter sequencing extended equipment life
  • Fault detection improved — anomaly alerts flagged a malfunctioning condenser fan weeks before it would have caused a breakdown
  • Full visibility achieved — for the first time, the campus facilities team had a single dashboard showing real-time status of every HVAC zone

Why this matters for Indian institutions: Most universities, hospitals, and government buildings in India have similar HVAC infrastructure — aging equipment, no intelligence layer, and rising energy costs. The IIT Jammu deployment proves that 20%+ savings are achievable without a single rupee spent on new equipment.

Technology Behind the Results

The SAAR AI-BMS is built on a combination of model-predictive control (MPC), reinforcement learning, and rule-based safety overrides. It is not a "set and forget" system — it continuously re-trains on new operational data, adapting to changes in building use, occupancy patterns, and seasonal climate shifts.

Because SAAR is incubated at IIT Jammu, it has been designed for Indian climatic conditions, power quality realities (voltage fluctuations, frequent outages), and the heterogeneous equipment mix typical of Indian institutional buildings. This is not Western HVAC software adapted for India — it is built for India from the ground up.

What's Next for IIT Jammu

Following the success of Phase 1, AE Systems and IIT Jammu are in discussions to expand the deployment to additional buildings on campus — including new academic blocks under construction — and to integrate the BMS data with the campus's energy metering infrastructure for full-building energy management.

The project also serves as a live research testbed: IIT Jammu faculty and students in energy systems engineering are using the deployment's data for academic research on building energy optimization in Indian climatic conditions.
